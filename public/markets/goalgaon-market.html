ABOUT THIS PLACE

One village, two halves.

Goalgaon is a rural settlement immediately north of Dalkhola, and it is recorded as two census villages: Purba Goalgaon (east) and Paschim Goalgaon (west), codes 309383 and 309384. Both sit in Dalkhola–I gram panchayat of Karandighi block, under Dalkhola police station, with PIN 733201 through Dalkhola sub-office. Local addresses are written both as “Vill – Goalgaon, P.O. Dalkhola” and as “P.O. Shikarpur, P.S. Dalkhola”.

The two halves are small and close together — about 700 m apart on the published coordinates — so for anyone travelling or trading they work as one place. Together they held 487 households and 2,758 people at the 2011 census. Neighbouring settlements include Hatbari, Shikarpur, Pukurchala and Bhusamoni.

The village grew out of the refugee settlement belt around Dalkhola. The town's recorded history notes that after Partition, families arriving from East Bengal settled in the surrounding rural areas — naming Hatbari, “Goalgaon (Madrasa)”, “Goalgaon (West)”, Shikarpur, Pukurchala and Bhusamoni among them. Goalgaon has been a farming village on the edge of a market town ever since.

The pattern is a working farming village, not a trading one: 782 of 2,758 people are recorded as workers (28.4%), and cultivators plus agricultural labourers account for about two in five of them. Female literacy trails male literacy sharply in both halves (56.7% against 77.3% in Purba, 55.3% against 79.6% in Paschim), which is typical of the rural blocks in this district.

THE TRADE

Farming here, trading in Dalkhola

Goalgaon's economy is agricultural, and the market it feeds is next door. Dalkhola is described in regional references as an important centre for the maize, jute, oil and grain trade of North Dinajpur, with maize grown across the border in Bihar and sold on this side — which makes the town a collection and forwarding point rather than a farm market.

  • Paddy, maize and jute — the crops of this block. Census figures put 146 cultivators and 179 agricultural labourers across the two Goalgaon villages, with the rest of the workforce in other trades or marginal work.Census 2011
  • Selling into Dalkhola — growers here are close enough to the town to sell directly at its arats and market lanes rather than through a village haat.
  • Rice milling — parboiled rice mills operate in and around Dalkhola. No official count is published, so this guide prints no number.
  • Banking and services — no bank branch is documented in the village. A business-correspondent banking point has been listed at Paschim Goalgaon, P.O. Shikarpur, P.S. Dalkhola, which is how many households here reach banking.
  • Retail — daily-needs shops in the village and along the lanes towards Hatbari Road serve households that would otherwise walk into town.

Landmarks that can be checked

  • Paschim Goalgaon S.S.K. — a Shishu Shiksha Kendra (state pre-primary/primary centre) recorded in Dalkhola–I gram panchayat.
  • The Goalgaon madrasah — local historical records list “Goalgaon (Madrasa)” as one of the settlement areas around Dalkhola; the madrasah is what gave the spot that name.
  • Village lanes to Hatbari and Shikarpur — the two adjoining settlements, both also in Dalkhola–I GP.
What this guide does not claim. No shop names, phone numbers, opening hours, mill counts or daily market rates are listed, because none could be checked against a reliable source. The Dalkhola “second largest grain market in West Bengal” line that circulates online is likewise left out — it has no traceable source.

Finding the area

Goalgaon lies about 2 km north of Dalkhola town centre, on the rural side of the municipality. Come off NH-27 in the town and follow the village lanes north past Hatbari.

ACCESS

How to reach Goalgaon

Everything routes through Dalkhola, where the highway and the railway meet. The village is a short drive north of the town, on the same side as Hatbari.

  • By road — NH-27 (Porbandar–Silchar) runs through Dalkhola, where NH-12 and NH-17 also meet. Older signboards and shop addresses may still say NH-31 or NH-34, the pre-renumbering names.
  • By train — Dalkhola station (code DLK) is on the Katihar–Siliguri line under Northeast Frontier Railway’s Katihar division, with 3 platforms and a history going back to 1871. The station is the closest railhead to the village.
  • By air — Bagdogra (IXB) is the region’s commercial airport, further along NH-27 past Islampur towards Siliguri. Kishanganj, across the border in Bihar, has no commercial airport.

LOCAL INFORMATION

What to confirm before you trade

This guide lists no shop names, phone numbers or opening hours, because none could be checked against a reliable source. Four things are worth confirming in person or by phone:

  • Whether a haat sits here — no market day is published for Goalgaon or for this block. Ask Dalkhola–I gram panchayat or Dalkhola Municipality (03525-257650).
  • Mandi rates — maize and paddy prices change daily. Check an official agricultural marketing price bulletin, then confirm with the arat or trader you intend to deal with.
  • Shop and mill timings — most trade in Dalkhola happens in the morning; rice mills run their own seasonal schedules.
  • Whether a business still exists — directory listings for this area go stale quickly, so call ahead.

If you run a shop, mill or transport service in Goalgaon and want a verified listing here, send the details to Dalkhola Online so they can be checked before publishing.
